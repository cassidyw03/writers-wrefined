# Chapter 4: Black Dye

Haskett's guards came to Tallow Street the next morning with a drawing of Wren's face.

It was not a very good drawing, but it did not need to be. The guards were stopping every girl with red hair, and there were not many people in Veyle with hair as red as Wren's. Tobias saw the drawing nailed to the door of the fish market and ran all the way to the infirmary to warn her.

"They say you're a thief," he panted. "They say you helped Lady Seraphine sell the crown. There's a reward. Ten silver."

"Ten silver," Wren said. "That's insulting."

"Wren," Mira said.

"I'm just saying. I'd have turned myself in for twenty."

Mira did not laugh. She went to the back of the infirmary and came back with a clay jar and a pair of linen gloves.

"Walnut hulls and iron salts," she said. "I use it for dyeing bandages. It will also dye hair. Sit down."

Wren looked at the jar. Then she looked at the drawing that Tobias had pulled off the market door. Then she sat down.

It took an hour. The dye stank like rotten nuts and burned her scalp, and Mira worked it into every strand while Soot watched from the windowsill with deep disapproval. When it was done, Mira rinsed it out in a basin and handed Wren a hand mirror.

Wren's red hair was gone. Her hair was black now, as black as tar, and the girl in the mirror looked like somebody else's cousin. She cut it short at the jaw with Mira's surgical scissors, so that it would not be the same shape either.

"It won't wash out," Mira said. "Not for months. You'll have to let it grow."

"Good," Wren said. She touched the ends of it. It felt like someone else's hair. "Good."

---

Brother Edric's desk in the Drowned Library was covered with maps when they came down the stair that afternoon. Orrin was already there, leaning on a shelf to rest his peg leg. He looked at Wren's black hair, raised his white eyebrows, and said nothing at all, which from Orrin was a compliment.

"Come," Edric said. "Look."

He unrolled a large chart and pinned the corners with brass weights. His fingers moved across the raised ink as he talked, tracing coastlines and rivers he had never seen.

"Here is Veyle," he said, touching a spot at the edge of the map. "The westernmost port in the kingdom, here on the western coast where the sun sets over the harbor. Here is the coast road. Here is the Pass. The Grey Hand used to keep a hideout in the old watchtower at the top of the Pass, here, before the King's men burned them out. If I were hiding something for six weeks, I would go back to a place I knew."

"Then that's where we go," Orrin said.

Wren leaned over the map. "Brother Edric, what does the crown look like up close? So I know it if I see it."

"A plain silver circlet," Edric said, "set with seven pearls, one for each of the seven old provinces of the coast. Small pearls. Yellowed with age. You would walk past it in a market stall and think it was a cheap copy."

"Seven pearls," Wren repeated, fixing it in her mind.

---

They would leave at dawn. Orrin went back to the ship. Tobias went with him, because he had nowhere else to sleep and because, Wren suspected, he liked the stew.

Wren walked home to Tallow Street through the back alleys with her short black hair under a fisherman's cap. Two of Haskett's guards passed her at the corner of the rope walk, and neither of them looked twice. She felt a fierce little thrill of victory, and then she felt tired.

At home, she sat at the kitchen table and took out paper and a stub of pencil. She was going to the Pass, and she did not know how long she would be gone, and she thought she ought to write to her mother. Her mother kept a bakery in Hollowmere, three days inland, and she worried when she did not get a letter every week. Wren wrote that she was well, that Mira was well, that the cat was fat, and that she would be traveling for the Guild for a little while.

She did not write anything about the crown.

Mira came in from the infirmary late, smelling of willow bark. She looked at the letter on the table and then at Wren's black hair.

"It suits you," she said.

"It does not."

"No," Mira agreed. "It doesn't. Come home safe."
