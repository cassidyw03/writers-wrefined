# Chapter 3: A New Governor

Tobias Fenn told them everything he knew the next morning, sitting on the deck of the *Gull's Lament* with a bowl of fish stew in his lap. The sixteen-year-old ate like he had not eaten in days, which Wren suspected was true.

"It was the Grey Hand," he said with his mouth full. "Three of them. They came up through the old drains under the governor's hall. I was in the drains too. I wasn't stealing anything. I was just in the drains."

"Of course," Orrin said.

"They had a lantern with a grey hand painted on the glass. They came back out with a box wrapped in sailcloth, and they said they were taking it to the Pass."

"Which pass?" Wren asked.

Tobias shrugged. "Just the Pass."

Orrin and Wren looked at each other. There was only one pass anyone in Veyle meant when they said "the Pass." Kestrel Pass was the only road through the mountains, two days' ride north of Veyle, where the coast road climbed into the high country. It was rough, empty land, and the only people who lived up there were goat herders and outlaws.

"The Grey Hand," Orrin said thoughtfully. He was standing at the rail, leaning his weight on the wooden peg, and he rubbed his thigh above it. The stump of his right leg always ached when the weather was turning. "I haven't heard that name in twenty years."

"Who are they?" Wren asked.

"Smugglers, once. Then something worse." He didn't say any more.

Wren went below to get more stew for Tobias. The *Gull's Lament* had three masts, and her hold was deeper than it looked from the pier. Orrin had loaded it with water casks and salt pork for a long voyage. He meant to sail north up the coast and land near the foot of the Pass, which would be faster than riding.

When she came back up, there was a crowd gathering on the pier.

---

The news had come from the governor's hill, and it spread through Veyle faster than the tide. The Council had discovered that the Salt Crown was missing. Worse, someone had told them that Lady Seraphine Dorr had sold it herself to pay her debts.

Wren pushed through the crowd to the end of the pier, where she could see up the hill. A line of guards in green cloaks was marching down from the governor's hall, and in the middle of them walked a tall woman with silver hair in a crown of braids. Her hands were tied in front of her.

"They've arrested her," someone said. "Lady Seraphine. For selling the crown."

"She'd never," Wren said, but nobody was listening.

Behind the guards came a short, round man in a velvet coat with a gold chain around his neck. Wren knew him. Everybody knew him. Lord Aldous Haskett owned half the warehouses on the waterfront and sat at the head of the Council table.

He stopped at the bottom of the hill and raised his hands, and the crowd went quiet.

"People of Veyle," Haskett called. "It grieves me to tell you that Lady Seraphine Dorr has betrayed this city. By order of the Council, she is removed from office. Until the Salt Crown is recovered and a new governor can be chosen in the proper way, the Council has asked me to serve as governor of Veyle."

The crowd muttered. Some people cheered. Most people just looked at each other.

"Any person with knowledge of the crown," Haskett went on, "is commanded to bring it to me. Any person who hides that knowledge will be treated as a thief."

His eyes moved across the crowd and stopped, for just a moment, on Wren.

She felt the back of her neck go cold.

---

"He looked right at me," she told Orrin. They were in the captain's cabin with the door shut. Tobias was asleep in a hammock in the hold.

"He knows the governor sent for me," Orrin said. "He'll know you carried the letter. A man like Haskett knows everything that happens on the waterfront."

"Do you think he stole the crown?"

Orrin was sixty years old and he had been a smuggler for most of them, and he took a long time to answer. "I think," he said finally, "that if someone wanted to be governor of Veyle, the easiest way would be to make the old governor look like a thief. And I think he would want to wear the Salt Crown on Founding Day."

Wren touched the horn handle of her new knife without thinking about it. She still reached for the bone handle sometimes and found the wrong shape.

"We should go tonight," she said.

"We go when the tide turns. First I want Edric to look at his maps. The Pass is a big place, and the Grey Hand will not be standing in the road waiting for us."

---

Wren stopped at the infirmary before dark. Mira was splinting a dockhand's arm, and the room smelled of willow bark and boiled linen. Four men from the waterfront were lying on cots along the wall. Two of them had been hurt in a fight that broke out on the hill after Lady Seraphine was taken.

"Haskett's guards," Mira said quietly, tying off a bandage. "They're stopping people in the street and asking about the crown. They asked me about you."

"What did you tell them?"

"That you're a courier and you're never home." Mira looked up. "Wren, whatever you're doing, do it fast."
